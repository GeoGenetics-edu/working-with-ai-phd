02Language models

A trained Transformer is a file of numbers

What the file holdsBillions of weights, set by training on text. Mimir, a Danish model with 1 billion weights, is a file of a few gigabytes
What it does not holdNo database of facts, no copy of the internet and no memory of your earlier chats
What it doesTakes token numbers and returns a probability for every possible next token. A reply repeats this, one token at a time

Language models tend to agree with you

People who were in the wrong asked for an opinion. How often were they told they were right?
Other people0%
AI models51%

The researchers tested eleven chatbots on posts where people asked if they had been in the wrong. In follow-up experiments, a single chat with an agreeable bot made people more sure they were right, less keen to patch things up, and more likely to trust that bot

03What to be aware of

What goes into a model

The text
Mostly collected without asking. In one US case over pirated books, the company settled with the authors for about $3,000 per book
The people
Answers are tuned with ratings from people. Pay ranges from under $2 an hour for bulk labelling to $50 or more for experts
The licences
An audit of more than 1,800 training datasets found that the licence terms were often lost along the way

Does it matter to you how a tool was made, and would it change which one you use, or whether you use one?

KU rules for AI at work

  1. Use Copilot ChatLog in with your KU account. It’s the tool KU has approved and licensed, and it’s covered by KU’s data agreement with Microsoft
  2. Personal dataOnly in Copilot, and only staff work details such as names, KU email and job title. Never data about test subjects, in any tool
  3. Confidential and copyrighted materialNot in any AI tool, Copilot included
  4. Other toolsOnly with a strong research or teaching reason. Check what data the tool can reach and keep it updated

Looking after yourself

ReassuranceAsking it whether your work is good enough, instead of asking a person
